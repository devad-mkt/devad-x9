# Copywriting

Create persuasive copy that makes one audience feel, understand, believe, and
act. Combine article-led narrative depth with landing-page conversion
architecture. Do not let templates flatten the human story.

## Route by Mode

Choose one primary mode before working:

| Mode | Use when | Main output |
|---|---|---|
| `PLAN` | Strategy or evidence must be settled first | brief, source map, page argument, section plan |
| `CRAFT` | Writing new copy from a brief or sources | complete copy with CTA and image plan |
| `REWRITE` | Existing copy has useful substance but weak delivery | revised copy plus material-change notes |
| `AUDIT` | The user wants diagnosis, scoring, or prioritized fixes | ranked findings and replacement examples |
| `BUILD` | Copy must be placed in HTML or a limited editor | valid preview or CMS-ready block sequence |
| `FUNDRAISING` | The page asks donors to support a human cause | ethical emotional appeal, proof, repeated donation path |

For mixed requests, use `PLAN -> CRAFT/REWRITE -> BUILD -> AUDIT`. Keep each
gate compact; gates prevent guesswork and do not justify process-only delays.

Read [modes-and-gates.md](references/modes-and-gates.md) for the full router.
For fundraising or emotion-led work, also read
[emotional-conversion.md](references/emotional-conversion.md). For pages with
several images, read [image-storytelling.md](references/image-storytelling.md).
Load [use-cases.md](references/use-cases.md) only for the matching page type.

## Gate 1: Pre-Plan

Bind these facts before outlining:

- page purpose and the one primary action;
- audience, awareness, traffic source, motivations, fears, and objections;
- offer or cause, mechanism, benefits, proof, and meaningful constraints;
- editor, platform, format, length, mobile, and asset limitations;
- source authority and what must not be claimed;
- success condition: what the reader should feel, know, believe, and do.

If information is missing, inspect supplied sources first. Ask only when the
answer materially changes the offer, risk, or result. Otherwise mark the
assumption and continue.

## Gate 2: Evidence

Build a claim ledger before persuasive prose. Classify every consequential
claim:

- `VERIFIED`: supported by current primary or owner-approved evidence;
- `PROVIDED`: supplied by the user but not independently verified;
- `PLACEHOLDER`: clearly marked for later confirmation;
- `DROP`: unnecessary, misleading, unprovable, or outside authority.

Distinguish observed visuals from operational facts. A photograph can show
water bottles, a truck, or a person; it cannot alone prove beneficiary counts,
location, delivery completion, ongoing impact, or use of funds. Verify
time-sensitive claims. Never invent prices, outcomes, testimonials, urgency,
scarcity, certifications, or guarantees.

## Gate 3: Pre-Craft

Choose:

1. one audience;
2. one big promise or emotional truth;
3. one primary CTA and destination;
4. one human scene the reader can picture;
5. the proof order;
6. CTA cadence;
7. image sequence and caption purpose;
8. the objections that genuinely block action.

Outline the page as an argument, not a pile of sections:

`stakes -> desired change -> mechanism -> proof -> deeper meaning -> action`

For a fundraising page, article-led storytelling usually carries the emotional
case better than a generic landing-page formula. Use landing-page rules to
shape hierarchy and CTA placement around that narrative.

## Gate 4: Pre-Code

Use only when implementing or previewing copy:

- inventory the editor's supported blocks, links, images, headings, and limits;
- inspect nearby files, existing styles, asset paths, and current changes;
- decide the smallest implementation that accurately previews the final editor;
- preserve user-owned work and avoid speculative components;
- prepare meaningful alt text and responsive image behavior.

Do not build interactions the target editor cannot support. A static semantic
preview is enough when the real deliverable is copy, image order, and
readability.

## Craft the Copy

Lead with the outcome or human stake. Make the reader picture a real moment
without presenting an imagined scene as a documented beneficiary story.

Prefer:

- clear, specific language over clever abstraction;
- benefits and consequences over feature lists;
- concrete verbs and nouns over inflated adjectives;
- one idea per section;
- informative headings that advance the argument;
- donor or customer language over internal project language;
- relevant facts, mechanisms, and proof near the claim they support;
- a sincere voice that matches the audience's culture and intent.

The cause, reader, customer, or product is the hero. The organization is the
credible guide. Emotional copy may be intense, but never humiliating,
coercive, exploitative, or based on fabricated intimacy.

Never publish internal narration such as “the reference images show,” “the
campaign materials explain,” “saved snapshot evidence,” or editorial comments
about why a section exists.

## Place Calls to Action

Surface the primary CTA after the reader understands the first meaningful
reason to act. Repeat the same action after major conviction peaks and at the
close. Vary supporting CTA language only when the destination and action remain
clear.

Do not place a broad FAQ or a heading such as “Questions before you give”
between the emotional case and the main close. It frames donation as hesitation
and interrupts momentum. Resolve one true blocking objection beside the
relevant CTA; place additional details after the principal close.

## Use Images as Part of the Argument

Each image must do at least one job: establish stakes, show the mechanism,
provide proof, reveal the result, or deepen human meaning. Captions are
mini-ads: they interpret why the moment matters to the reader.

Track source, rights, consent, date, location, and sensitivity. If consent or
context is uncertain, prefer non-identifying, adult, contextual, or
organization-owned imagery and report the gap before publication.

## Edit in Separate Sweeps

Do not attempt one vague “make it better” pass. Run:

1. `CLARITY`: can every sentence be understood once?
2. `VOICE`: does it sound like this organization and audience?
3. `SO-WHAT`: does each section explain why the reader should care?
4. `PROOF`: is every claim supported, scoped, and placed correctly?
5. `SPECIFICITY`: can vague language become concrete?
6. `EMOTION`: can the reader picture the human consequence?
7. `FRICTION`: is anything delaying, confusing, or weakening the CTA?
8. `ANTI-SLOP`: remove clichés, robotic transitions, hedging stacks,
   repetitive rhythms, empty grandeur, and fake sophistication.

Anti-slop is a final quality sweep, not the strategy. Do not mechanically ban
all adverbs, em dashes, rhetorical questions, or short sentences when they
serve voice and rhythm.

## Publish Gate

Before delivery:

- title, opening, promise, CTA, and destination agree;
- strongest emotional and factual claims pass the claim ledger;
- no placeholder or internal instruction leaked into public copy;
- FAQ and transparency support rather than dominate conversion;
- image order, captions, alt text, rights, and consent are checked;
- links, heading hierarchy, mobile width, and image loading are verified;
- the final ask is specific, visible, and emotionally earned;
- unresolved facts and publishing decisions are listed separately.

When the user asks for implementation, validate the actual artifact rather than
claiming success from prose alone.
