# Emotional Conversion

## Ethical Emotional Intensity

Make emotion honest, vivid, and connected to action.

Use:

- one imaginable moment;
- a concrete daily-life consequence;
- the emotional burden carried by the affected person;
- a credible path from the reader's action to relief;
- dignity, agency, and shared humanity.

Avoid invented stories or quotations, humiliating imagery, savior language,
guilt threats, false urgency, guaranteed impact, and shock without purpose.

Signal imagined scenes with “Imagine…” or “For a parent, this can mean…”.
They are scenarios, not case evidence.

## Fundraising Argument

1. **Human stake:** a moment the donor can picture.
2. **Why it matters:** the daily and emotional consequence.
3. **What the organization does:** concrete mechanism.
4. **First ask:** a clear donation invitation.
5. **Meaning of the gift:** what changes for a person or family.
6. **Visible chain:** preparation, delivery, access, or service.
7. **Proof:** sourced facts, images, partners, or receipts.
8. **Deeper value:** faith, dignity, community, or continuing benefit.
9. **Final close:** restate the choice and ask.
10. **Additional details:** concise FAQ, fund-use note, sharing option.

The first CTA follows the first complete reason to act. Repeat after a major
proof or meaning section and at the close.

## FAQ Placement

Use FAQ only for real objections: recipient, mechanism, small-gift relevance,
fund use, or non-donation support.

Do not write “Questions before you buy/give?” or “Not sure yet?” before the
main close. These headings rehearse hesitation. Use “Additional details” or
answer one blocker beside the relevant CTA.

Keep answers specific. If evidence cannot support a useful answer, omit it or
name what the organization must confirm.

## Faith-Sensitive Appeals

- use faith language sincerely and sparingly;
- connect the act to service, intention, mercy, or continuing benefit;
- do not promise divine reward, acceptance, or a guaranteed spiritual result;
- verify quotations and religious attributions;
- do not use sacred language as decorative conversion garnish.

## WFA Water-Appeal Lessons

- Article-led narrative carried emotion better than generic LP modules.
- LP rules still improved hierarchy, CTA cadence, and objection placement.
- Excessive caution weakened the appeal when caveats occupied the emotional
  path. Keep necessary transparency concise and late.
- “Campaign materials show” is internal evidence narration, not public copy.
- The strongest opening helps the reader picture a cup, child, meal, or
  parent's uncertainty.
- A visible chain of work connects donation to action without inventing a
  guaranteed individual outcome.
- FAQ belongs after the main close unless one answer unlocks action.
- A few purposeful emojis aid warmth and scanning; a wall of them trivializes
  serious need.
