# Conversion Copy Rules

Merged from the `copywriting` skill (emotional conversion, gates, image argument, editing sweeps). Applies in addition to all type workflows when the conversion layer is active. Technical and data-only tasks skip this layer entirely.

## Pre-Plan Gate

Bind these facts before outlining:

- page purpose and the one primary action;
- audience, awareness, traffic source, motivations, fears, objections;
- offer or cause, mechanism, benefits, proof, meaningful constraints;
- editor, platform, format, length, mobile, asset limitations;
- source authority and what must not be claimed;
- success condition: what the reader should feel, know, believe, do.

If information is missing, inspect supplied sources first. Ask only when the answer materially changes the offer, risk, or result. Otherwise mark the assumption and continue.

## Evidence Gate: Claim Ledger

Classify every consequential claim before persuasive prose:

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| VERIFIED | Supported by current primary or owner-approved evidence |
| PROVIDED | Supplied by user but not independently verified |
| PLACEHOLDER | Clearly marked for later confirmation |
| DROP | Unnecessary, misleading, unprovable, or outside authority |

Distinguish observed visuals from operational facts. A photograph can show water bottles, a truck, or a person; it cannot alone prove beneficiary counts, location, delivery completion, ongoing impact, or use of funds. Verify time-sensitive claims. Never invent prices, outcomes, testimonials, urgency, scarcity, certifications, or guarantees.

## Pre-Craft Gate

Choose before writing:

1. one audience;
2. one big promise or emotional truth;
3. one primary CTA and destination;
4. one human scene the reader can picture;
5. the proof order;
6. CTA cadence;
7. image sequence and caption purpose;
8. the objections that genuinely block action.

Outline the page as an argument, not a pile of sections:
`stakes -> desired change -> mechanism -> proof -> deeper meaning -> action`

For fundraising pages, article-led storytelling carries the emotional case better than a generic landing-page formula. Use landing-page rules to shape hierarchy and CTA placement around that narrative.

## Craft the Copy

Lead with the outcome or human stake. Make the reader picture a real moment without presenting an imagined scene as a documented beneficiary story.

- clear, specific language over clever abstraction;
- benefits and consequences over feature lists;
- concrete verbs and nouns over inflated adjectives;
- one idea per section;
- informative headings that advance the argument;
- donor or customer language over internal project language;
- relevant facts, mechanisms, and proof near the claim they support;
- a sincere voice that matches the audience's culture and intent.

The cause, reader, customer, or product is the hero. The organization is the credible guide. Emotional copy may be intense, but never humiliating, coercive, exploitative, or based on fabricated intimacy.

Never publish internal narration such as "the reference images show," "the campaign materials explain," "saved snapshot evidence," or editorial comments about why a section exists.

## CTA Cadence

Surface the primary CTA after the reader understands the first complete reason to act. Repeat the same action after major conviction peaks and at the close. Vary supporting CTA language only when the destination and action remain clear.

Do not place a broad FAQ or a heading such as "Questions before you give" between the emotional case and the main close. It frames the decision as hesitation and interrupts momentum. Resolve one true blocking objection beside the relevant CTA; place additional details after the principal close.

## Image Storytelling

Each image must do at least one job: establish stakes, show the mechanism, provide proof, reveal the result, or deepen human meaning. Captions are mini-ads: they interpret why the moment matters to the reader.

| Role | Question it answers |
|------|---------------------|
| Stakes | Who or what is affected? |
| Context | Where does the need or use occur? |
| Mechanism | What work, product, or service happens? |
| Proof | What credible evidence supports the claim? |
| Outcome | What relief, result, or transformation is visible? |
| Close | What human meaning should remain before action? |

Track source, rights, consent, date, location, and sensitivity. If consent or context is uncertain, prefer non-identifying, adult, contextual, or organization-owned imagery and report the gap before publication.

## Editing Sweeps

Do not attempt one vague "make it better" pass. Run:

1. CLARITY: can every sentence be understood once?
2. VOICE: does it sound like this organization and audience?
3. SO-WHAT: does each section explain why the reader should care?
4. PROOF: is every claim supported, scoped, and placed correctly?
5. SPECIFICITY: can vague language become concrete?
6. EMOTION: can the reader picture the human consequence?
7. FRICTION: is anything delaying, confusing, or weakening the CTA?
8. ANTI-SLOP: remove clichés, robotic transitions, hedging stacks, repetitive rhythms, empty grandeur, and fake sophistication.

Anti-slop is a final quality sweep, not the strategy. Do not mechanically ban all adverbs, em dashes, rhetorical questions, or short sentences when they serve voice and rhythm.

## Ethical Emotional Intensity

Make emotion honest, vivid, and connected to action.

- one imaginable moment;
- a concrete daily-life consequence;
- the emotional burden carried by the affected person;
- a credible path from the reader's action to relief;
- dignity, agency, and shared humanity.

Avoid invented stories or quotations, humiliating imagery, savior language, guilt threats, false urgency, guaranteed impact, and shock without purpose.

Signal imagined scenes with "Imagine..." or "For a parent, this can mean..." or "For a family...". These are scenarios, not case evidence.

## Faith-Sensitive Appeals

- use faith language sincerely and sparingly;
- connect the act to service, intention, mercy, or continuing benefit;
- do not promise divine reward, acceptance, or a guaranteed spiritual result;
- verify quotations and religious attributions;
- do not use sacred language as decorative conversion garnish.

## Source Ladder

Prefer:
1. current organization records and approved campaign facts;
2. current live page or authenticated source;
3. supplied official documents and approved prior copy;
4. primary public sources;
5. dated saved snapshots;
6. competitor/reference pages for structure only.

Competitor copy is inspiration, not evidence and not text to imitate.

## Gate Outcomes

- PASS: enough evidence and clarity to continue.
- PASS_WITH_ASSUMPTIONS: assumptions are low-risk and recorded.
- DEPENDENT_BLOCK: one claim or section cannot proceed; other work can.
- STOP: missing authority, rights, or a decision would materially change the result.

A gate is a decision point, not a mandatory new document.
